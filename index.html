I work on federal fiscal policy. As a Tobin Pre-Doctoral Fellow at The Budget Lab at Yale University, I build and maintain the microsimulation models behind our estimates of what tax and transfer proposals cost, who pays for them, and how they land across the income distribution — analysis produced for congressional offices, journalists, and the public rather than for a journal.

Before Yale I did policy analysis from several sides of the process. At the SEC's Division of Economic and Risk Analysis I built the foreign-private-issuer dataset behind a white paper that informed disclosure rulemaking. At the Carl Vinson Institute of Government I benchmarked rural Georgia labor markets and turned the results into county-level development strategy for local officials. In the Georgia House I drafted memoranda on tax and energy bills through a full legislative session, and at the State Department's Global Engagement Center I analyzed foreign influence operations for embassy distribution. I graduated from the University of Georgia as a Foundation Fellow with an A.B. in Economics and Political Science.

Policy interests: federal tax and transfer policy, safety-net design and distributional analysis, and how AI is reshaping labor markets and the institutions that have to absorb the risk.
