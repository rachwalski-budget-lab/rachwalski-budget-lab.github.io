I'm a Pre-Doctoral Fellow at The Budget Lab at Yale University, where I build and maintain the microsimulation models behind our estimates of what federal tax and transfer proposals cost, who pays for them, and how they land across the income distribution. The work runs on legislative timelines: it goes to congressional offices, committee staff, journalists, and the public rather than into a journal.

I came to it through government. At the SEC's Division of Economic and Risk Analysis I built the foreign-private-issuer dataset behind a white paper that informed disclosure rulemaking. At the State Department's Global Engagement Center I analyzed foreign influence operations for embassy distribution. In the Georgia House I drafted memoranda on tax and energy bills through a full legislative session, and at the Carl Vinson Institute of Government I benchmarked rural Georgia labor markets into county-level development strategy for local officials. I graduated from the University of Georgia as a Foundation Fellow with an A.B. in Economics and Political Science.

Policy interests: federal tax and transfer policy, safety-net design and distributional analysis, and how AI is reshaping labor markets and the institutions that have to absorb the risk.
